# WHO CNS 5 Diffuse Glioma Classification and Multiparametric MRI Staging

## Diagnostic Definition and Pathophysiology
Diffuse gliomas constitute the most common primary intra-axial neuroepithelial neoplasms of the central nervous system. Under the WHO Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System (5th Edition, WHO CNS 5), classification integrates molecular profiling (IDH1/2 mutation status, 1p/19q codeletion, ATRX loss, TERT promoter mutation, CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion) with histological features.
- **Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype (WHO Grade 4)**: Hallmark features include microvascular proliferation, pseudopalisading necrosis, and aggressive parenchymal infiltration.
- **Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant (WHO Grade 2, 3, or 4)**: Graded based on mitotic activity and CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion.
- **Oligodendroglioma, IDH-mutant and 1p/19q-codeleted (WHO Grade 2 or 3)**.

## Neuroimaging Hallmarks (Multiparametric MRI)
1. **T1-Weighted Post-Gadolinium**:
   - High-grade gliomas characteristically demonstrate thick, irregular, nodular ring enhancement surrounding a central non-enhancing hypointense necrotic core.
   - Low-grade gliomas typically demonstrate minimal or no contrast enhancement.
2. **T2/FLAIR Hyperintensity**:
   - Extensive non-enhancing infiltrative signal abnormality representing a combination of vasogenic edema and non-enhancing infiltrative neoplastic glial cells.
   - Distinct from pure vasogenic edema; infiltrative glioma cells extend well beyond visible contrast enhancement into adjacent white matter tracts.
3. **Mass Effect and Hemispheric Asymmetry**:
   - Compression of adjacent sulci, ipsilateral lateral ventricle effacement, midline shift (>5 mm predicts herniation risk), and subfalcine or uncal herniation vectors.
4. **Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI/ADC)**:
   - High cellularity zones demonstrate restricted water diffusion with hypointensity on Apparent Diffusion Coefficient (ADC) maps.
5. **Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy (MRS)**:
   - Markedly elevated Choline (Cho) to N-acetylaspartate (NAA) ratio (Cho/NAA > 2.0), reduced NAA (neuronal loss), and elevated Lipid-Lactate peak reflecting anaerobic metabolism/necrosis.

## Clinical Symptom Presentation
- **Progressive Headache**: Morning worsening, valsalva exacerbation, signs of increased intracranial pressure (ICP).
- **New-Onset Epileptic Seizures**: Focal seizures with or without secondary generalization; common presenting sign in cortical and subcortical locations.
- **Focal Neurological Deficits**: Hemiparesis, dysphasia/aphasia, sensory disturbances corresponding to lesion topography.
- **Cognitive and Executive Decline**: Frontal lobe syndrome, psychomotor slowing, apathy, acoustic/phonetic speech pauses.

## Clinical Decision Support Directives
1. Urgently schedule 3-Tesla volumetric neuronavigation MRI with multiplanar thin-slice T1+C, T2/FLAIR, DWI, and perfusion (DSC-MRI rCBV).
2. Initiate corticosteroid therapy (Dexamethasone) for symptomatic mass effect and perilesional vasogenic edema with gastric prophylaxis.
3. Urgent Neurosurgical Oncology evaluation for stereotactic biopsy vs maximal safe resection with intraoperative neuromonitoring (fMRI / DTI tractography).
4. Neurological consultation for anti-seizure medication (ASM) initiation if seizures or high epileptogenic location.
