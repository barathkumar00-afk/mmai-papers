## Ground-glass opacity

Ground-glass opacity describes a region of increased lung attenuation that
**does not obscure underlying bronchial and vascular markings**. This single
distinguishing feature separates it from consolidation, which obscures them.

Important limitation: ground-glass attenuation is a *descriptive imaging
pattern*, not a diagnosis. The same appearance arises from many processes —
infection, interstitial inflammation, interstitial oedema, haemorrhage,
aspiration, and artefact such as underpenetration or motion.

## Distribution

- **Bilateral, peripheral, subpleural, basilar** distribution: seen in acute
  interstitial processes and in many viral pneumonias; also described in acute
  eosinophilic injury and in some non-infectious interstitial conditions.
- **Bilateral, diffuse, central/perihilar** ("bat-wing") distribution: more
  typical of alveolar oedema.
- **Focal lobar consolidation**: typical of bacterial pneumonia but not specific.

Distribution is a *probability-shaping* observation. It changes the probability
of a differential; it does not establish one.

## Cardiomegaly

An increased cardiothoracic ratio suggests cardiogenic aetiology, but projection
and inspiratory effort significantly affect the measurement. Portable
supine films commonly exaggerate the ratio.

## Pleural effusions

Small bilateral effusions are common in both cardiogenic and non-cardiogenic
processes. Large unilateral effusions are more suggestive of a unilateral
process (parapneumonic, malignant, or congestive).

## Technical adequacy

Before any pattern is described, image adequacy must be assessed:

1. Rotation — the spinous processes should be equidistant from the medial
   clavicular heads.
2. Inspiration — at least 8-10 posterior ribs should be visible.
3. Penetration — thoracic vertebrae should be just visible through the heart.
4. Collimation and motion.

An inadequate study limits the strength of any description, and the limitation
should be stated explicitly rather than silently interpreted.

## What a radiograph cannot establish

A single projection cannot establish temporal change, depth, enhancement
behaviour, or the nature of an underlying process. Comparison with prior
imaging and cross-sectional imaging are frequently required.
