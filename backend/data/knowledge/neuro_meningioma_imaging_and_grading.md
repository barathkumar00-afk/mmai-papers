# Meningioma Grading, Morphologic Radiomics, and Dural Attachment Diagnostics

## Diagnostic Definition and Epidemiology
Meningiomas are the most frequent primary extra-axial non-glial intracranial neoplasms, originating from meningothelial arachnoid cap cells of the arachnoid villi.
- **WHO Grade 1 (Benign)**: ~80-85% of cases (Meningothelial, fibrous, transitional subtypes); slow-growing, circumscribed.
- **WHO Grade 2 (Atypical)**: ~15-20% of cases (Increased mitoses >= 4/10 HPF, spontaneous necrosis, sheeting, prominent nucleoli, brain invasion); higher recurrence rate.
- **WHO Grade 3 (Anaplastic/Malignant)**: ~1-3% of cases (Rhabdoid, papillary, frank sarcomatous/carcinomatous morphology, >= 20 mitoses/10 HPF).

## Neuroimaging Characteristics (MRI & CT)
1. **Extra-Axial Signs**:
   - Broad-based dural attachment with the classic pathognomonic **"Dural Tail Sign"** (thickening and avid enhancement of adjacent tapering dura mater).
   - CSF cleft sign (thin rim of cerebrospinal fluid interposed between the mass and displaced cerebral parenchyma).
   - Cortical buckling and inward displacement of cortical gray matter and pial vessels.
2. **Signal Characteristics**:
   - **T1-Weighted**: Iso- to slightly hypointense relative to cortex.
   - **T2/FLAIR**: Iso- to hyperintense. Hyperintensity on T2 often correlates with increased vascularity or soft/syncytial subtype.
   - **Post-Contrast T1**: Intensely, uniformly, and homogeneously enhancing mass with sharply circumscribed boundaries.
3. **Bony Remodeling (CT/MRI)**:
   - Hyperostosis of adjacent cranial vault (particularly in convex, parasagittal, sphenoid wing, and olfactory groove meningiomas).
   - Bony erosion or osteolysis is more suggestive of atypical (Grade 2) or malignant (Grade 3) histology.
4. **Perilesional Edema**:
   - Variable vasogenic edema in adjacent brain parenchyma, driven by vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) secretion and cortical pial supply recruitment.

## Differential Diagnosis
- Dural metastases (prostate, breast, melanoma).
- Solitary fibrous tumor / Hemangiopericytoma (lack of hyperostosis, prominent flow voids, staghorn vasculature).
- Schwannoma (cerebellopontine angle location with expansion of internal auditory canal).
- Neurosarcoidosis / Pachymeningitis.

## Clinical Recommendations & Protocol
1. Contrast-enhanced MRI with high-resolution volumetric 3D T1-SPGR/MPRAGE and coronal reconstruction.
2. Assess venous sinus patency (MR Venography / CTV) if tumor abuts superior sagittal sinus or transverse/sigmoid sinus (Simpson Grade planning).
3. Observation with serial volumetric imaging for asymptomatic, small, calcified incidental Grade 1 meningiomas.
4. Neurosurgical resection (maximal safe Simpson resection) or stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS/Gamma Knife) for enlarging, symptomatic, or skull base lesions with cranial nerve impingement.
