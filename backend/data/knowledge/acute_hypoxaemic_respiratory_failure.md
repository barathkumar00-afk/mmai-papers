## Definition

Acute hypoxaemic respiratory failure is a state of inadequate oxygenation
(commonly defined as PaO2 < 60 mmHg or SpO2 < 90%) with elevated work of
breathing, developing over hours to days. It is distinguished from chronic
respiratory failure by the abrupt onset and by the absence of a chronic
compensatory pattern.

## Assessment

Oxygenation is assessed by the PaO2/FiO2 (P/F) ratio:

- P/F > 300: no acute lung injury
- P/F 200-300 with PEEP >= 5 cmH2O: mild acute respiratory distress
- P/F 100-200 with PEEP >= 5 cmH2O: moderate acute respiratory distress
- P/F <= 100 with PEEP >= 5 cmH2O: severe acute respiratory distress

The P/F ratio requires an arterial (or reliably arterialised) PaO2 and an
inspired oxygen fraction. A saturation-based SpO2/FIO2 ratio is a reasonable
screening surrogate in some settings but is not equivalent to the P/F ratio and
has different calibration limits.

## Differentiating the mechanism

Two broad mechanism groups must be separated:

1. Cardiogenic (hydrostatic) pulmonary oedema — raised left atrial pressure.
   Supporting features include cardiomegaly, perihilar alveolar distribution,
   Kerley B lines, pleural effusions, and elevated natriuretic peptides.
2. Non-cardiogenic oedema — increased capillary permeability. Supporting features
   include a normal cardiac silhouette, peripheral or basal ground-glass
   attenuation, absence of Kerley B lines, and preserved ventricular indices.

No single feature is definitive. Interpretation must combine the physiological
data, the imaging appearance, and the clinical trajectory.

## Inflammatory markers

Serum C-reactive protein is a non-specific acute-phase reactant. An elevated
value indicates an inflammatory response but does not identify its cause, and it
does not by itself distinguish infectious from inflammatory from non-inflammatory
processes. It is useful for trend monitoring, not for diagnosis.

## Cardiac injury markers

A normal troponin at presentation reduces the likelihood of acute myocardial
injury but does not exclude it: early presenters may still be troponin-negative
before the biomarker rises, and a normal value carries no exclusionary weight
after several hours of ischaemia.

## Escalation

Sustained hypoxaemia despite oxygen, increasing work of breathing, or
haemodynamic instability warrants urgent senior and critical-care review. Oxygen
therapy targets are individualised; a common working target in adults without
known hypercapnic respiratory failure is SpO2 92-96%.
