# Pituitary Neuroendocrine Tumors (PitNET) and Sellar Region Pathologies

## Diagnostic Definition and Classification
Pituitary adenomas, classified in current nomenclature as Pituitary Neuroendocrine Tumors (PitNETs), originate from adenohypophyseal cells of the anterior pituitary gland within the sella turcica.
- **Classification by Size**:
  - **Microadenoma**: Maximum diameter < 10 mm (confined to sella, rarely produces mass effect, presents via endocrine hypersecretion).
  - **Macroadenoma**: Maximum diameter >= 10 mm (often extends suprasellar into optic chiasm or parasellar into cavernous sinus).
  - **Giant Adenoma**: Maximum diameter >= 40 mm.
- **Classification by Functional Status**:
  - Non-functioning pituitary adenomas (NFPA): ~30-40% (silent gonadotroph; present with mass effect).
  - Prolactinomas (PRL-secreting): ~40-50% (galactorrhea, amenorrhea, hypogonadism).
  - Somatotroph adenomas (GH-secreting): ~10-15% (acromegaly / gigantism).
  - Corticotroph adenomas (ACTH-secreting): ~5-10% (Cushing disease).
  - Thyrotroph adenomas (TSH-secreting): <1%.

## Multiparametric Sellar MRI Protocol
1. **Dedicated Thin-Slice Sellar Protocol**:
   - High-resolution coronal and sagittal T1- and T2-weighted sequences (<= 2 mm slice thickness).
   - Dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE) coronal T1 imaging with early arterial phase to detect microadenomas (which enhance more slowly than normal pituitary gland).
2. **Macroadenoma Appearance**:
   - Expansion and ballooning of the bony sella turcica with remodeling of the sellar floor.
   - Suprasellar extension through the diaphragma sellae creating a characteristic **"Figure-of-8" or "Snowman" sign** due to waist compression.
   - Upward displacement, stretching, and thinning of the optic chiasm.
3. **Cavernous Sinus Infiltration (Knosp Classification)**:
   - Evaluated by tumor relation to the internal carotid artery (ICA) flow voids:
     - Knosp Grade 0-1: Medial to intercarotid tangent line (low invasion probability).
     - Knosp Grade 2: Crosses intercarotid tangent but medial to lateral tangent.
     - Knosp Grade 3-4: Encases ICA with total lateral extension (predicts subtotal resection).
4. **Pituitary Apoplexy**:
   - Acute infarction or hemorrhage within a preexisting adenoma: intrinsic T1 hyperintensity (methemoglobin), fluid-debris levels, acute severe headache, and cranial nerve III/IV/VI palsies.

## Visual and Endocrine Consequences
- **Bitemporal Hemianopsia**: Classic visual field deficit resulting from selective compression of crossing nasal retinal fibers in the optic chiasm.
- **Optic Neuropathy**: Reduced visual acuity, afferent pupillary defect, optic disc pallor.
- **Hypopituitarism**: Secondary adrenal insufficiency, central hypothyroidism, gonadotropin deficiency.

## Clinical Protocol & Recommendations
1. Dedicated high-resolution pituitary protocol MRI (coronal dynamic T1, thin T2, post-gadolinium).
2. Formal automated visual field testing (Humphrey / Goldmann visual perimetry).
3. Complete baseline pituitary hormonal panel (Serum Prolactin, IGF-1, 8 AM Cortisol, ACTH, Free T4, TSH, LH, FSH, Testosterone/Estradiol).
4. If prolactinoma is confirmed, medical therapy with dopamine agonists (Cabergoline) is initial first-line therapy.
5. Endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgical decompression for chiasmatic compression, visual field compromise, apoplexy, or non-prolactinoma macroadenomas.
