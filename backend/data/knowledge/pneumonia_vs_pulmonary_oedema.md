# Pneumonia and Pulmonary Oedema — Differential Discrimination

## The core problem

Pneumonia and cardiogenic pulmonary oedema are both common causes of acute
bilateral opacities with hypoxaemia, and they frequently overlap. Discriminating
them is one of the highest-value judgements in acute chest medicine, and one of
the most frequently made incorrectly.

## Findings favouring cardiogenic oedema

- Cardiomegaly (projection-sensitive; over-estimated on supine AP films)
- Perihilar, central "bat-wing" alveolar distribution
- Kerley B lines, subpleural septal thickening, fissural thickening
- Pleural effusions
- Elevated natriuretic peptides
- Clinical volume overload: raised JVP, peripheral oedema, positive fluid balance
- Known left ventricular dysfunction, ischaemic heart disease, valvular disease

## Findings favouring pneumonia

- Focal or multifocal lobar consolidation
- Air bronchograms within an opaque lobe
- Fever, pleuritic pain, focal crepitations
- Productive cough, sputum purulence
- Elevated inflammatory markers with a compatible syndrome
- Raised white cell count with a leftward shift

## Findings that do not discriminate

- Bilateral infiltrates: present in both
- Hypoxaemia: present in both
- Elevated CRP: present in both, and in many other inflammatory conditions
- Ground-glass attenuation: present in both
- Older age: present in both
- Reduced lung expansion: present in both

## The strongest discriminators

The discriminators that survive scrutiny are the *distributional* ones —
cardiac silhouette size, perihilar vs peripheral distribution, septal lines,
effusions — supported by clinical volume status. Biomarkers discriminate least.

## Overlap

"Mixed" patterns are common. A patient with ischaemic heart disease may develop
aspiration pneumonia; a patient with pneumonia may develop acute kidney injury
and volume overload. Forcing every presentation into a single label is a
recognisable failure mode.

## Practice point

Where the clinical and radiological pictures disagree, escalate rather than
resolve artificially. Serial imaging and clinical reassessment carry more
information than a single cross-sectional moment.
